Eratosthenes is one of the most renowned Greek mathematicians. He lived in the third century BC, (276 BC - 194 BC), and is best known for calculating the Earth's circumference. He was also a geographer, and a philosopher, but today we will discuss one of his mathematical findings.

Image of Eratosthenes

Today, we will remake his sieve in code.

The idea of the sieve is simply to find all the prime numbers between 1 and any specified number.

The magic lies in the fact it isn't a complicated mathematical equation. Instead it's an algorithm.

By the way, to this very day, nobody has managed to find an algebraic method to find prime numbers. Maybe you'll be the one who finds it though. Nothing is impossible.

Let's take a look at the sieve.

We already touched on the idea of the sieve in the last lesson.

The sieve works by making a list of all the numbers we want to check (for simplicity let's take the range 1 - 100), and then mark every composite number. Everything that isn't marked HAS to be prime.

Well, perhaps that's a little complicated. Let's give you an example.

We start with number 1. We already know it isn't a prime, so we skip it and go to 2.

Number 2 wasn't marked, so it is a prime. Every other multiple of 2 is composite though, so we rule every even number other than 2

We go to the next unmarked number 3. 3 is prime, and every multiple of 3 will be composite, so we rule them all out.

The next unmarked one will be 5, and we keep going like that.
